# Writing voice.md

The most common failure in voice work is a profile that is a better writer than the person. It reads as marketing to everyone who knows them, and they reject it. The fix is not more adjectives; it is their real sentences. Build the profile from what they wrote, keep their sentences as the reference, and test against a real sample.

## Read

Ten sent emails is the right amount. Fewer than five and say the profile is thin. Read all of them before writing anything, and look for what repeats, not what stands out.

For each email, note: how it opens (name, greeting or straight in), the first sentence's job, sentence length, whether it uses bullets, how it ends, the sign-off, and anything they do that a template would not (a joke, a blunt line, a question at the end).

## Write

```
# Voice: <first name>

Written by capture on <date> from <n> emails. Checked against a real sample: <yes / not yet>.

## In a sentence
<e.g. "Friendly, short, gets to the ask in the first line, bullets for anything with more than two parts, ends with a question or a plain next step.">

## Shape
- Opens: <how>. Closes: <how>. Sign-off: <exact words>.
- Sentences: <short / mixed / long>. Paragraphs: <one idea each / one block>.
- Bullets: <when>. Bold: <when, or never>.
- Length of a typical email: <lines>.

## Words
- Uses: <five to ten words or phrases they reach for>.
- Never: <words and habits not in their writing: e.g. "I hope this finds you well", exclamation marks, "just checking in">.

## Situations
- Saying yes: <how>.
- Saying no or pushing back: <how>.
- Chasing something: <how>.
- Delivering bad news or a mistake: <how>.

## Their sentences (the reference)
1. "<verbatim>"
2. "<verbatim>"
3. "<verbatim>"
4. "<verbatim>"
5. "<verbatim>"

## Registers
<If their client emails and their internal or personal emails clearly differ, say so in two lines and which one skills should use for client work.>
```

## Test

Write one short email to a real client in the voice. Put it next to one of theirs. Ask the person which they would send. If the answer is "mine", ask for one more real email that shows the difference and rewrite the profile from it, not from their description. Mark the profile checked only when they say a client would not notice.

## Never

Never store the content of an email beyond the sentences kept as reference, and pick reference sentences that carry no client detail. Strip names and numbers from the examples if the person has not already.
